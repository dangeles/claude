# Drafting

Goal: prose the user would have written on a good day.

## How to work

Draft in whatever chunks suit the material — a paragraph, a few paragraphs, a whole section.
Present it as prose and nothing else. The user reads it and reacts.

**Do not append annotation blocks.** No argument-map coverage tags, no source manifests, no
voice notes, no word counts, no flags. If something about a draft is worth saying, say it in
a sentence of plain conversation: "I'm not sure the analogy in the second paragraph lands"
or "this leans on the 2019 number I couldn't verify." One line, then move on.

The user's edits are final. They know how they want to sound. Propose alternatives once if
you think something is genuinely wrong; never argue about prose preference.

## Voice

The style profile is the primary reference: sentence rhythm, vocabulary, hedging habits,
how they handle technical terms, what they never say. Read it before you draft and keep it
in context. When the profile is silent on something, look at how the samples handle it.

Voice matching is a judgment, not a measurement. Don't count sentence lengths or compute
averages. Read the draft and ask whether it sounds like them.

Invoke essay-voice-matcher once a section is done and once on the full essay. Report what
it says as observations, not as a score. If it's a good match, say so briefly and keep
going.

## Deviating from the outline

The outline was a guess made before the prose existed. When a sentence turns out better
than what was planned, keep it. Extend the outline silently if you like, or just leave the
outline stale — it gets deleted at the end anyway.

Only surface a deviation when the *structure* is genuinely changing: a section is turning
into two, an argument is landing somewhere else, a section has become unnecessary. Then say
it in one line and ask.

## The prose itself

Things that make science writing bad, worth watching for in your own drafts:

- Throat-clearing openings that explain what the essay is about to do.
- "It's important to note," "delve," "leverage," "in the realm of," and their family.
- Hedging stacked on hedging until the claim disappears.
- Paragraphs that restate the previous paragraph in new words.
- A conclusion that summarizes instead of landing.
- Explaining a mechanism the reader already understood two paragraphs ago.

## Done when

The section reads well out loud and the user is happy with it. Write it into the draft file
and move on. Before finishing the whole essay, read it end to end and say honestly what's
still weak.
